# Movie Quality as a Sociotechnical Consumption Event

### An Open Research Project toward a PhD in Communication / Media Studies

-----

## Overview

This repository documents an open-source research project exploring how the **technical and social conditions of home streaming** shape audience judgments of movie quality. The project is in its early planning stages, with the long-term goal of producing PhD-level research.

The core insight: **people do not simply rate movies — they rate movie experiences.** Those experiences are shaped by display quality, audio fidelity, network stability, interruptions, co-viewing, room conditions, and attention. Audience ratings may therefore be noisy measures of film quality because they reflect not only the movie itself, but also the sociotechnical conditions of consumption.

-----

## Research Question

> To what extent are audience judgments of movie quality shaped by the technical and social conditions of home streaming consumption?

-----

## Thesis Statement

Audience judgments of movie quality are shaped not only by the intrinsic properties of films but also by the sociotechnical conditions of home streaming, including presentation quality, network stability, and social viewing context.

-----

## Theoretical Framework

Movie watching should be treated as a **sociotechnical consumption event** — jointly produced by:

- Content (the film itself)
- Platform (Netflix, Disney+, etc.)
- Device (TV, laptop, phone)
- Infrastructure (network stability, bandwidth)
- Environment (room, lighting, audio setup)
- Social context (solo, co-viewing, interruptions)

### Disciplinary Lens

- **Primary:** Communication / Media Studies
- **Secondary:** Sociology, Psychology, Consumer Behavior, Information Systems

-----

## Hypotheses

1. Better presentation quality (display resolution, audio fidelity) increases perceived movie quality.
1. Audio quality may matter as much as or more than screen size.
1. Buffering and unstable streaming reduce ratings through both degraded playback and viewer annoyance.
1. Interruptions and multitasking lower immersion and can make films seem weaker or slower.
1. Viewers may **misattribute** setup problems to the intrinsic quality of the movie.

-----

## Proposed Study Design

A **mixed-methods study**:

1. **Survey** — Ask viewers about a recent at-home movie experience and the conditions under which they watched it (device, audio setup, network, interruptions, co-viewers, etc.)
1. **Follow-up Interviews** — Explore what viewers believed they were actually evaluating when they assigned a rating.

-----

## Original Contribution

Audience ratings on platforms like Letterboxd, IMDb, and Rotten Tomatoes are widely treated as measures of film quality. This research argues they are also measures of **viewing conditions** — and that these two signals are currently entangled and unmeasured.
